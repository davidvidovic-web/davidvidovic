## The idea

I grew up fascinated by *Hackers* and its vision of cyberspace. Years later, I wanted to revisit that feeling by turning the film's mainframe sequence into a place people could explore for themselves.

The result is a browser-based 3D experience: a flight through a city of glowing data structures, animated code and colour, accompanied by music inspired by the film. It started as a personal tribute to a movie that helped spark my interest in technology.

## Building the mainframe

I used Three.js and custom GLSL shaders to build the environment, combining procedurally generated structures with effects such as falling text, glowing circuit floors and retro plasma. The experience runs in the browser and supports keyboard and mouse controls on desktop, as well as touch controls on mobile.

Exploration also became a game. Players can search for a hidden “Garbage File” and submit their time to a global leaderboard. Serverless functions and a Turso database support the leaderboard, while a music player gives visitors control over the soundtrack.

## The response

The project found an audience well beyond the original idea. It was shared across developer communities and reached 30,000 players in two days. People contributed music from extended cuts, discussed the film and helped test the experience. It was later played at a *Hackers* 31st-anniversary watch party.
